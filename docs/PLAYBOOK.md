# Operating Playbook: Content, Channels, Sales, Pricing and Membership

Status: synthesis, 13 October 2026. Built from a review of 16 UK neighbourhood and
town newsletters, the leading US local-newsletter operators (6AM City, Naptown
Scoop, Scottsdale Scoop, Axios Local, Charlotte Ledger, Lookout Santa Cruz, Patch),
and newsletter growth research. All figures are from search snippets of the cited
sources, not primary pages; items marked [NV] are unverified. Sources at the end.

The document has three parts: what the UK neighbourhood models actually do (section
1), what works nationally and internationally (section 2), and the design we adopt
for The Common, with the reasoning (sections 3 to 9).

**The common formula of the fast growers** (Wimble 0 to 10k in ~2 years, Edinburgh
Minute 0 to 25k in ~2 years, Cheapskate 4k to 18k+, Local Authority to 500 payers):

1. A fixed send slot that is never missed. Consistency is the product.
2. One utility the reader cannot get elsewhere: a real lead story plus a curated
   to-do list (Wimble), the ten links that matter (Minute), two free events a day
   (Cheapskate), opinionated council coverage (Local Authority).
3. A named human voice and a reply-able address; reader-sourced items.
4. Funding decided before launch: an anchor sponsor (Wimble) or a paid tier from
   day one with the core kept free (Minute, Local Authority).
5. The physical world as the growth engine: a 20k print run around a city-defining
   event, hustings with 550 attendees, a launch party, brand giveaways. None of the
   UK growers used paid acquisition.
6. A network platform first (Substack) and a migration to Ghost or beehiiv at
   roughly 10k to escape fees.

**Gaps nobody in the UK set fills** (our openings):

- No self-serve ad booking anywhere, not even Neighbour Net with its public rates.
- No searchable events calendar or business directory behind any fast grower.
- No WhatsApp Channel found for any title [NV].
- Small titles lose cadence; AI drafting removes that failure mode.
- Only The Wimble blends civic and lifestyle. The others pick one. Battersea and
  Clapham have both in abundance.
- Legacy incumbents are voiceless and dated yet still paid. A modern,
  personality-led title can take that budget.
- Nobody owns Balham or Nine Elms editorially.

## 2. What works nationally and internationally

### 2a. Content

- **No partisan politics, no crime as a beat.** 6AM City (1m+ subscribers, 45 to
  50% opens), Naptown Scoop (65% opens) and Scottsdale Scoop all run this rule; it
  is also the brand-safety pitch to advertisers. Civic news (planning, transport,
  schools, parks) is in; party politics and named-person crime are out.
- **Short, scannable morning issue of 750 to 1,000 words**: one original item of
  300 to 500 words plus curated listings. 6AM's own data. Restaurant and food items
  get the most clicks (Naptown).
- **Service content converts to paid, civic news does not.** Ottawa Lookout's paid
  tier is restaurant reviews, curated event guides and lifestyle picks. Boerne Life
  Plus adds a Saturday edition. Mill adds mid-week long reads.
- **Reader participation**: polls, "hopes for 2026", birthday shoutouts, best-of
  votes. Cheap to run and the most shared items.

### 2b. Cadence and send time

- Naptown Scoop runs Monday/Wednesday/Friday profitably at 65% opens; 6AM and Axios
  Local are daily; UK growers are weekly Thursday or daily 7am. MailerLite data shows
  weekly senders open at 33% vs daily at 30%; no isolated 3x/week data.
- beehiiv platform data: 5am sends open worst (~23%), 10am best (~43%); very early
  sends get buried. Effect sizes are small. Test 6:30 vs 7:30 vs 5:30pm on clicks.
- Inventory logic: 3 issues x 3 slots is roughly 10 units a week, matching
  Naptown's 10 slots. Fewer issues means scarcer slots, which is how Margate sells
  out ten weeks ahead at 3k subscribers.

### 2c. Growth

- **Referral programmes**: Morning Brew attributes ~30% of subscribers to referrals.
  Naptown's realistic numbers: only ~10% of readers ever refer anyone, 0.1% hit the
  second milestone, one superfan produced 653 referrals. Design rules: first
  milestone at 3 referrals (a shoutout), about $1 of reward value per referral, mug
  and tote at higher tiers, a permanent share block in every issue. A private
  Facebook group as a reward failed.
- **Paid Meta as seed, then off**: Naptown spent roughly $21k on Facebook ads over
  two years and stopped at ~15k when CAC rose; Scottsdale and Ottawa Lookout did
  the same. Arlington Bagel got under $1 per subscriber using Instagram Stories and
  Reels placements only, a 10-mile radius and a simple voiceover video of the
  newsletter; Stories and Reels run 50 to 70% cheaper than feed. UK agencies quote
  £0.65 to £1.60 for local sign-ups. No UK local newsletter reports using paid
  acquisition, so treat it as a test, not a plan.
- **Instagram is top of funnel and the sponsor-prospecting channel**: Scottsdale's
  first advertiser came from a cold DM; Reels for reach, Stories link sticker for
  conversion; a Winnipeg operator sees 15 to 20% sign-up lift during consistent
  posting runs.
- **Facebook groups are unreliable**: posts deleted, operators banned, admins
  charging. Reply helpfully in threads, drop links only when relevant. Reddit
  weekly event threads worked for Scottsdale. Nextdoor's publisher programme was
  Jacksonville Today's third-largest organic source.
- **Weekly free giveaway** (Cheapskate: 10k to 36k with no paid promotion), but
  track 90-day retention of giveaway cohorts; one Substack purged 40% of giveaway
  sign-ups as dead.
- **Welcome sequence**: three emails beat one; welcome emails click at ~16% vs ~4%
  for regular sends.
- **Avoid recommendation networks and co-registration** for a geographically
  bounded list: untargeted, and retained-subscriber cost is 2 to 4x the headline.
- **Segment by acquisition source** and cut channels whose 90-day retention lags
  (Ottawa Lookout; 1440 finds paid-social subscribers open at half the rate).

### 2d. Monetisation

- **Flat-rate local slots, not CPM.** Every local operator sells per-send or
  per-week with bundle and annual-prepay discounts. Back Bay Buzz (3k subscribers,
  2x/week, the closest analogue to a London neighbourhood list): primary $200 per
  send or $380 per week, secondary $100 or $180, sponsored bullet in the weekend
  agenda $75, Instagram story add-on $40. 6AM City (40k to 60k per city): text ad
  $300 to $500, mini article from $1,000, header $500 to $1,000, social post $500 to
  $1,000, bundles 20% off. Naptown: 4 ad types, 10 slots, top unit ~$812, billed
  ~$15k a month at 18k subscribers (~$0.85 per subscriber per month).
- **A stable roster of annual sponsors beats one-offs.** Charlotte Agenda took 70% of
  revenue from ~30 annual sponsors and refused lead-gen buyers; Axios Local copied
  it. Local advertisers started buying in earnest once a 6AM city passed ~20k.
- **Reporting is a product feature.** Scottsdale's live sponsor portal and
  post-issue report; 6AM's one-week report. Sponsors renew when they see numbers.
- **Job board** pays for the tooling with one or two listings a month (Wichita Life
  $99 / $199 featured); jobs were ~10% of Charlotte Agenda revenue.
- **Events**: small ticketed socials earn £300 to £500 plus a sponsor (Scottsdale
  happy hour, Naptown scavenger hunt); Axios says events do not scale. Treat them as
  marketing and member perks, not revenue.
- **Mix**: ad-led locals sit at 60 to 85% ads (6AM 85%, Lookout Santa Cruz 60/40,
  Charlotte Agenda 70% sponsorship). Paywall-first locals (Mill 92%, Charlotte
  Ledger 85%) are long-form journalism products, a different business.

### 2e. Paid tier design

- What converting local tiers actually offer: an extra service edition (Saturday
  guide, restaurant reviews), member events (happy hours, trivia, walks), a local
  business discount card (Boerne Life, The Batavian, Friends & Farmers at 35
  businesses), birthday shoutouts, member giveaways, and a "support" framing (Axios:
  perks "are not why you should join"; Lookout lets members send 10% of dues to a
  local nonprofit and over 90% opt in).
- Ad-free as a perk appears in no local example. Ads stay for members.
- Prices: UK £5 to £8.95 a month (Mill £8.95, Local Authority £6, Leytonstoner £5,
  Don't Miss £4, Edinburgh Minute £45/year). US $8 to $17 a month.
- Conversion: platform medians 0.6% to 3%; local lifestyle lists plan at 1 to 2%;
  3 to 5% only with a genuinely gated must-read edition; London Centric's ~10% is
  investigative city journalism, not a comparable.
- Founding-member launches (Mill 10% lock-in, Boerne lifetime seats) work but no
  conversion data is published.

### 2f. Failure modes

1. Burnout from 7-day operation (Lincoln County Dispatch, 250News). Mitigation:
   fixed cadence, templates, AI drafting, one part-timer who can cover.
2. Cadence slip, the first visible symptom of burnout.
3. Thin or concentrated ad base (Local News Glasgow, Nuneaton Post closed; Willingham
   lost 11 advertisers at once). Target ~30 recurring sponsors, none above 15 to
   20% of revenue.
4. Paywalling too early or paywalling the utility (Dallas Morning News, Latterly;
   Don't Miss Margate's gated event lists).
5. Over-expansion ahead of unit economics (6AM cut 11 of 30 markets in 2025; Axios
   Local paused after missing its 2022 forecast).
6. Audience-quality traps from giveaways and recommendation networks.
7. Platform dependence for distribution (Facebook group bans).
8. Commoditisation by AI aggregation: Patch runs AI newsletters in 14,000
   communities; 6AM bought 350+ AI-generated town letters. Pure link roundups have
   no moat. Original reporting, voice and presence do.

## 3. Our design: content

**Positioning.** "The Common: Battersea, Clapham, Nine Elms & Balham. What's
happening, what's opening, what's on. Twice a week, from a neighbour." Civic and
lifestyle blended (the Wimble lane, which nobody else in SW London occupies), no
party politics, no named-person crime, Battersea named first.

**Editorial rules.**
- One reported lead per main issue, 300 to 400 words, alternating civic (Nine Elms
  masterplan, Northcote Road pedestrianisation, Clapham Common events licensing,
  Northern line, school places) and lifestyle (openings, people, history).
- Openings and closings are the most clicked and most shared items: run them every
  issue with a photo, and break them first (this is also where editorial backlinks
  from Hot Dinners and Time Out come from).
- The weekend to-do list is the retention hook: 8 to 10 dated items, one line each,
  linking to the web calendar. Never list sold-out events.
- Reader corner every issue: a poll, a question, a reply of the week.
- Crime: no names, no identifying detail, no mugshots, and only when it changes
  what readers do (road closures, scams targeting the area).
- Claude drafts every section from the ingest brief in the house voice; the editor
  approves every issue. Nothing goes out unreviewed.

**Quantity.** Main issue 900 to 1,200 words with 6 to 8 photos, 5-minute read.
Brief 350 to 500 words, text-led, 2-minute read.

## 4. Our design: cadence and channels

**Cadence: launch at two sends a week, grow to three.**

| Send | Slot | Content | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Common** (main) | Thursday 07:00 | Lead, in brief, openings, weekend to-do list, sponsor of the week, reader corner, noticeboard | The proven UK slot (Wimble, S.E. London); readers plan weekends; advertisers want Thursday |
| **The Week Ahead** (brief) | Monday 07:00 | Planning decisions, council meetings, roadworks and TfL, school dates, "three things this week" | Low cost with AI drafting; Edinburgh Minute-style utility; a second sponsor slot |
| Saturday "Where to go" (member edition) | Saturday 08:00, from month 4 | One long read, three restaurant or pub picks with a verdict, member perks | The service content that converts to paid (Ottawa Lookout, Boerne) |

Move to Monday/Wednesday/Friday when the list passes ~5k and Thursday inventory is
sold out two weeks ahead. Scarcity sells slots; a third issue is added for demand,
not for ambition. Test 07:00 against 06:30 and 17:30 on clicks in month two.

**Channels, in priority order** (revised: the operator is a cold-email specialist
and will not run Instagram for now).

1. **Email** is the product and the owned asset. A referral block in every issue.
2. **WhatsApp Channel**: mirror of the Thursday "5 things" and breaking openings.
   No competitor has one; zero production cost.
3. **Nextdoor** publisher programme once the domain has four weeks of output.
4. **Reddit**: a weekly "what's on in SW London this weekend" thread, mod-safe.
5. **Print as an event**: 10k to 20k free copies once or twice a year around
   Clapham Common festivals or Battersea Park fireworks (the Wimble play).
6. **Battersea Beat partnership**: buy a Story at launch, propose a monthly swap,
   keep the acquisition conversation warm (93k followers, no email product).
7. **Facebook groups**: helpful replies only.
8. **Instagram**: secure @thecommon / @thecommonlondon handles; post nothing for
   now. Revisit only as a sponsor-facing shop window if advertisers ask for it.
   The evidence (Edinburgh Minute, Cheapskate, Mill, London Centric, Naptown) is
   that growth comes from consistency, forwards, referrals, giveaways and physical
   moments, not from organic social.

## 5. Our design: outbound sales

**Sequence.**

1. **Anchor sponsor before launch** (the Wimble precedent). Targets in order:
   Battersea Power Station (needs local goodwill and footfall), The Junction BID
   and This Is Clapham BID (exist to promote levy payers), one estate-agency chain
   (Foxtons, KFH, Marsh & Parsons, Hamptons, Knight Frank, Savills all have offices
   in every village in the patch), one hospitality group. Offer a 12-month
   "brought to you by" in masthead, Instagram bio and archive at £1,500 to £3,000.
2. **Prospect list.** Google Places scrape of SW11/SW4/SW8/SW12 for 4+ star, 50+
   review businesses in discretionary categories; Companies House match to confirm
   Ltd status; enrich owner name and email from the website. Target 1,500+
   prospects, roughly 70% incorporated.
3. **Cold email to incorporated businesses** (lawful under PECR for corporate
   subscribers; identify yourself, offer opt-out, honour it). Three touches over ten
   days: (a) one paragraph with a live stat and the booking link; (b) an
   auto-generated mockup of their ad inside a real issue, Claude personalises the
   first line from their website; (c) a final nudge with a first-slot discount.
   Expect 1 to 3% to book.
4. **Email every new opening** the week we cover them, with the link to the item.
   The coverage is the warm intro (Scottsdale did this by Instagram DM; email
   works the same for incorporated businesses).
5. **Phone and in person for sole traders** (cafés, trades) where cold email is not
   lawful. One afternoon a week walking Northcote Road, Lavender Hill, Clapham High
   Street, Abbeville Road, Bedford Hill with printed cards and the media kit.
6. **Self-serve booking page** with Stripe for classifieds, event spotlights and
   featured listings. Nobody in the UK set offers this; it removes you from the
   small transactions.
7. **Monday report to every active sponsor** automatically: delivered, unique
   clicks, CTR vs issue average, next available dates. Renewal link in the report.
8. **Annual roster goal**: 30 recurring sponsors within 18 months, none above 15 to
   20% of revenue. Refuse pure lead-gen and anything the category blocklist covers
   (gambling, payday lending, unregulated health claims).

**Pitch content.** Subscribers, 30-day open rate (prune first), clicks per issue,
reader postcode mix, household profile (use ONS ward data), named sponsors,
reader testimonials. One-page PDF media kit regenerated monthly from live stats.

## 6. Our design: pricing

Anchors: Back Bay Buzz at 3k subscribers (primary $200, secondary $100, bullet $75,
Instagram story $40); ChiswickW4 advertorial £300+VAT to 19k; Luton Council slot
£250+VAT to 5k; Margate sells out at ~3k. SW London affluence supports the upper
half of each range once opens are proven.

| Product | 2k subs | 5k subs | 10k subs | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Presenting sponsor (top line + 60-word native item + image) | £150 | £250 | £350 | per issue; 4 issues -10%, 12 issues -20%, annual -25% |
| Sponsor of the week box with reader code (Thursday) | £120 | £200 | £300 | the Wimble format; one per issue |
| Secondary native item (40 words) | £75 | £130 | £180 | |
| Weekend to-do spotlight (featured event card) | £40 | £60 | £80 | self-serve |
| Noticeboard classified (25 words) | £20 | £30 | £40 | self-serve |
| Featured directory listing | £40/mo | £60/mo | £80/mo | self-serve, annual -20% |
| Job posting | £79 | £99 | £149 | 30 days on site + one issue mention; £199 featured |
| Instagram Story add-on | +£40 | +£60 | +£100 | |
| Dedicated send (max 2/month) | 2.3x presenting | | | |
| Anchor "brought to you by" (12 months) | £1,500 | £3,000 | £6,000 | masthead, bio, archive |

Rules: max two paid native items per main issue, every one labelled "Partner";
no category exclusivity under £5k a year; card or invoice via Stripe, 14 days;
founding packages paid up front. Reprice at each subscriber tier, never mid-contract.

**Revenue check at 8k subscribers, two issues a week (9 a month):** presenting
£300 x 9 x 60% = £1,620; sponsor of the week £250 x 4 x 80% = £800; secondary
£160 x 9 x 50% = £720; spotlights and classifieds ~£400; directory 15 x £70 =
£1,050; jobs 3 x £120 = £360; anchor £250/month amortised; membership 2% x 8k x
£5 = £800. Total roughly £6,000 a month per title before the third issue. Three
titles, or two at 12k, reach the £20k target; the third issue adds ~£2,000 per
title when demand justifies it.

## 7. Our design: free vs paid

- **The core is free forever**: Thursday main issue, Monday brief, calendar,
  directory, archive. Paywalling the utility is the single most repeated mistake in
  the UK set.
- **Supporter tier from month 4** (once past ~2,500 subscribers): £5 a month or
  £45 a year, founding cohort of 100 at £36 a year locked for life.
- **What members get** (service and belonging, not more news, and not ad-free):
  - Saturday "Where to go" edition: one long read and three honest picks.
  - The Common Card: discounts at 20+ partner businesses (we recruit them as a
    free sponsor benefit; it also gives sponsors a reason to join).
  - Monthly members' drinks or walk, sponsor-hosted.
  - Comments and topic votes; birthday shoutout on request.
  - 10% of dues to a local cause members choose each quarter (Lookout model).
  - First access to print editions and events.
- **Expect 1 to 2% conversion**, so membership is the second line, not the first.
  If the Saturday edition proves must-read, 3 to 5% is possible.
- **Framing**: "support local, get the good stuff", never "unlock the news".

## 8. What we do better than the field

| Field weakness | Our move |
|---|---|
| Email-only growers with no web product | Searchable events calendar and business directory at the same domain, auto-compiled, sold as featured listings, compounding in search |
| No self-serve ads anywhere | Stripe booking page for classifieds, spotlights, listings and jobs |
| No sponsor reporting | Automated Monday report and a live portal |
| Cadence slips in small titles | AI-drafted, human-approved issues on a fixed slot, with a cover plan |
| Civic or lifestyle, never both | The Wimble blend, in a patch with far more of both |
| No WhatsApp channel anywhere | WhatsApp Channel from day one; partnership with Battersea Beat for social reach instead of building our own |
| Voiceless incumbents (Neighbour Net) | A named editor who answers replies and walks the high streets |
| Balham and Nine Elms uncovered | Named in the strapline, dedicated hub pages |
| Battersea called "Clapham" | Battersea first, always |

## 9. Guardrails

- Never miss a send. Bank two evergreen issues before launch.
- Prune 90-day non-openers before publishing any open rate.
- No sponsor above 15 to 20% of revenue; 30 recurring sponsors is the goal.
- Segment by acquisition source; kill channels whose 90-day retention lags.
- Paid Meta only as a capped test (£1,500) on Stories and Reels placements in a
  3-mile radius; stop above £1.50 per subscriber.
- Add the third weekly issue only when Thursday inventory sells out two weeks ahead.
- Keep the core free. Charge for service, belonging and business visibility.
- Label every paid item. Keep a category blocklist. Keep the crime rules.
- Document the process so a part-timer can produce an issue from the brief alone.
